Need a hand, or found a bug (the software kind)? Open an issue on GitHub. A real person reads every one, and we aim to reply within two working days. You'll need a free GitHub account. Issues are public, so please don't include personal details. You can also browse existing issues to see if someone has asked already.

Playing

How do I play on iPhone Duo?

Turn the Duo sideways and hold it open like a book, with the fold running top to bottom and one hand on each half. Hold it relaxed and slightly folded, however feels natural: Snapjaw follows your own resting angle, so there's no exact angle to find. The two halves are the flytrap's lobes. When bugs are sipping nectar inside, snap the phone a little shut, about 22°, and the trap bites; then open it back to where you were and it's ready again. Your first go starts with First Bite, a short lesson that walks you through it.

How do I play on other iPhones?

Tap anywhere to snap. The trap opens again by itself a moment later. Everything in the game, including every achievement, works this way.

What's the trick?

Patience. Every bug inside the trap is caught in one bite, and the score is the bugs' points × the number of bugs × your chain, so a crowd is worth waiting for. Bugs sipping nectar (the gold ring) are distracted and easy to catch; bugs still flying are quick. Bugs right at the rim, by the teeth, react faster too. Catch 3 or more at once for a Feast and win snaps back. And never eat a bee.

The bugs flew off before I caught them

If you close the trap slowly, twitchy bugs notice and leave (you'll see the hint "Snap it — quick!"), and a very slow close doesn't bite at all. On a Duo, the snap is short, about 22°, so a small, brisk movement is all it takes, a bit like turning a page. There's no need to close it further or harder: the bite happens at 22°.

I bit a bee

It's let go, unharmed and a bit cross ("Bzzt!"), and it costs you an extra snap; a wasp stings and costs two. It also starts your chain again. Bees, bumblebees and wasps all wear a white halo so you can spot them. When one is sipping in the trap, wait for it to leave.

How do I get more snaps?

A run starts with 10 snaps and you can hold up to 12. Every snap costs one. A Feast (3 bugs or more in one bite) wins one back, 5 or more wins two, and a ladybird or the rare Golden Scarab brings luck too. When you're down to 0, your trap is tired and the run ends.

Is this OK for my hinge?

Yes. A snap is a short close of about 22° from a relaxed book pose, well inside the range the Duo's hinge is made for, and snaps are limited, so a run is only a few dozen of them. Snapjaw never asks you to close the phone, lay it flat or slam it, and closing further than the bite gains nothing. Never put anything real on the screen.

Half the screen went blurry for a moment

That's an iOS effect: when you start folding a Duo from fully flat, iOS briefly blurs one half of the screen for a second or two. It isn't Snapjaw and no app can switch it off. So Snapjaw keeps your score, snaps and hints on the half that stays sharp (the right half), and you feel a tick in your hands whenever a bug lands, wherever it is. If the other half stays sharp on your Duo, use Settings > HUD on the left. Holding the Duo slightly folded rather than fully flat avoids the blur altogether.

It says "Bend it a little, like a book"

Your Duo is open almost flat. Fold it in a little to a comfortable book pose. There's no penalty; it just plays best (and the screen stays sharp) from a slight fold.

The game paused and asks me to open it again

You folded the Duo much further than play needs (below about 50°, past an L shape). Open it back to your normal book pose and the run carries on. If you close it fully, the outer screen asks you to open it again.

It asks me to hold it open like a book

Snapjaw is played with the Duo held like a book, in landscape. If you hold it upright in portrait, or stand it on a table, it shows how to hold it.

How does the Daily Bog work?

The same bugs for everyone that day. Your first try of the day is the one that counts for the Daily Bog leaderboard; you can practise as much as you like afterwards. A new Daily Bog starts at midnight UTC (midnight UK time in winter).

The Bug Book and plant ranks

The Bug Book has 16 species, each with a drawing, a one-line joke and how many you've caught. A page fills in the first time you catch that bug; bees, bumblebees and wasps fill in the first time you spare one. Your plant rank, from Sprout to Bog Monarch, grows with the total number of bugs you've ever caught. Ranks are just for show: your trap's rim gets a little fancier. There are no purchases, ever.

Can I see the lesson again?

Yes: Settings > Replay First Bite.

Sound, feel and settings

No sound, or too much?

Snapjaw plays sound even with the silent switch on. To make it follow the silent switch, turn on Respect silent switch in Settings. Sound and Haptics each have their own switch.

Reduce motion

Settings > Reduce motion turns down the camera bump and screen effects when the trap bites. The bugs and the trap still move: that's the game.

Game Center

Optional. If you're signed in, your best Feed score goes to the Best Feast leaderboard, your counted Daily Bog try to the Daily Bog leaderboard, and achievements are reported as you earn them. Snapjaw works fully without it.

Purchases and your progress

Will I have to pay again later?

No. One price, no in-app purchases and no ads. Updates are free.

Family Sharing?

Yes, Snapjaw is available through Family Sharing.

Did I lose my progress?

Your progress lives only on your iPhone (and in your iPhone's own backups). Deleting the app deletes it, and we can't restore it, because we never had it.

Refunds

Purchases are handled by Apple. Request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Reporting a bug

Open an issue with:

  • your iPhone model and iOS version (Settings > General > About);
  • whether you were playing with the fold or by tapping;
  • the mode you were playing (Feed, Daily Bog or First Bite);
  • what happened, and what you expected (for a snap that fired too early or too late, roughly how far you had closed the phone);
  • a screen recording, if you can. It helps a lot.
