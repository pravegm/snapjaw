## The pitch (one paragraph)

**Snapjaw turns the iPhone Duo into a Venus flytrap.** Hold the Duo open like a book: the two halves are the trap's lobes and the hinge is its midrib. Bugs fly in and land on the glistening nectar to sip. Snap the phone a little shut and the trap bites with you, teeth clacking together, then open it again and wait. The trick is choosing when: a crowd caught in one bite multiplies your score by the number of bugs in it, but bees love nectar too, and you must never eat a bee. Snaps are what you spend, and big bites earn them back. Each run passes from morning to night with new bugs, from houseflies at breakfast to dragonflies at dusk and fireflies after dark, and a Bug Book of 16 species to fill as your plant climbs from Sprout to Bog Monarch. There's a Daily Bog with the same bugs for everyone, and a First Bite lesson that teaches by doing. On other iPhones, tap to snap. £1.99 / $1.99, no ads, no in-app purchases, no data collected, rated 4+.

**One-liner:** *Your iPhone Duo is a Venus flytrap: snap it a little shut on a crowd of bugs, and never eat a bee.*

## Key features (for writers who want bullets)

- **The phone is the plant.** A real Venus flytrap is two hinged lobes with teeth on the rim. So is an iPhone Duo held open like a book. The halves are the lobes, the hinge is the midrib, and the teeth on screen lean in as your hands close.
- **Choosing, not hitting.** Most of the game is waiting: two flies sipping, a third on the way, and a bee drifting toward the nectar. Bite now, or wait for the crowd? Score = the bugs' points × the number of bugs × the chain.
- **Never eat a bee.** Bees, bumblebees and wasps want the nectar too. A bee in the bite is let go, unharmed and cross ("Bzzt!"), and costs an extra snap; a wasp stings for two. Sparing them is half the skill.
- **Snaps are the resource.** 10 to start, every snap costs one, and a Feast (3 bugs or more in one bite) wins snaps back. When they run out, your trap is tired.
- **A day in the bog.** Every run moves from a gold morning to a blue night, and the cast changes with the light: ant lines and ladybirds by noon, moths and dragonflies at dusk, fireflies and crickets after dark, and now and then a Golden Scarab.
- **Kind to the hinge.** A snap is a short close of about 22° from however you like to hold the Duo. The bite is decided at 22°, so there's nothing to gain by closing further, and the game never asks you to close the phone or lay it flat.
- **It works around the Duo's blur.** iPhone Duo softens one half of the screen for a moment when a fold starts from flat; the score and snaps live on the half that stays sharp, and every landing also ticks in your hands.
- **The CHOMP.** A wet thwack, the teeth clacking together, a squelchy gulp that grows with the crowd, and the strongest haptic the phone has. All synthesised live.
