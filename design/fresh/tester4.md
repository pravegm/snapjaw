# Fresh player 4: "Helen", 45, a parent buying a game for the family (kids 8 and 11)

*She saw only the frames, in this order: the icon, Home 01, pose 31, lesson 04–09, play 10–19, 33–35, Results 20,
Daily 25, Results 26, Home 02–03, Bug Book 28–29, Settings 30, cover 32, play 21–24, then the 12 badges. She read no
docs.*

## What I think it is and how to play
- A cartoon Venus flytrap. Bugs land on it and you tap to make it shut and eat them.
- You want lots of bugs at once ("Wait for a crowd… now tap!"), and you must not eat the bees ("Never eat a bee.
  Wait…").
- The little red things at the bottom are your goes. You get some back for big bites, and when they run out, "Your
  trap is tired".
- There's a sticker book of bugs and a daily one.
- The score is called "protein", which made me laugh. For a second I thought it was a diet app.
- On Home there's a tiny picture of a folding phone (03), and the help screen (31) says "Hold it open like a book".
  So I think on the folding iPhone you close the phone a bit instead of tapping. My husband has one, and that would
  sell it to the kids.

## What confused me
- **The red squiggle icons at the bottom (05, 10).** They look like tiny mouths with teeth. I only knew they were
  "snaps" because the card in 09 drew a ring round them and said so.
- **The shut trap (13, 14, 18).** My first thought was a red pepper. My 11-year-old would say "it's a bum" and that
  would be the end of it. It's funny, but it isn't what you want on the main moment.
- **The night bee (15).** A pink box says "Bees cost a snap!" on top of some yellow writing I couldn't read
  ("Stung"?). The striped thing in the ring looked like a wasp to me. Did I lose a go or not?
- **The yellow "×2" pill with a chain link (11, 25, 35).** Nobody told me what it is.
- **The bottom of the closed-phone screen (32)** is cut off: "400 so far" is half missing.
- **The Bug Book (28)** has a little arrow sitting on top of the words.

## Would I pay £1.99?
**Yes.** Settings says "No ads, no purchases, no data collected". That sentence alone is worth £1.99 to me, after the
"free" games that ask my kids for money every two minutes. The bugs are sweet, the jokes in the Bug Book are kind
("Busy. Important. Absolutely not on the menu."), and nothing is gory.

## Would we play again?
The kids: yes, for the Bug Book (16 to collect) and the daily one. Me: a few goes. The runs looked short, and the
first one ended after a minute (Results 20: 21 bugs).

## "Feels finished": 7 / 10
It looks like a proper shop game: bright, glossy, consistent. Cut-off text (32), boxes on top of words (15, 25) and
the odd-looking shut trap are the things that make me think "not quite done".
